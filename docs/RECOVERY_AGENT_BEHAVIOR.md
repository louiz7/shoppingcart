# Recovery conversation and tool boundaries

The [Shopify knowledge inventory](RECOVERY_SHOPIFY_KNOWLEDGE.md) documents product details, public metafields/metaobjects, policies, permissions, caching and query limits.

GPT Live and its existing reasoning model interpret conversation intent. There are no runtime keyword lists, fixed acceptance phrases, or additional consent-classifier model calls. The voice prompt provides flexible guidance for objections, listening, interruptions and delegation. The founder persona remains disclosed as AI working for the founding team.

The reasoning agent selects `confirm_contact`, `send_checkout_link(use_discount=...)` and `stop_contact`. A scoped refusal (for example, declining a discount) does not automatically stop all contact. An actual contact stop must invoke `stop_contact` immediately. Discount creation alone never means acceptance; sending defaults to a plain link with the discount parameter removed.

The worker serializes committed LiveKit conversation messages and binds gated tool requests to the current user event. Event IDs are injected by the worker, never supplied by the model. The service checks call/shop ownership, recency, latest-user-event identity, active contact permission, current cart and approved offer, and rechecks authorization before provider dispatch. A discounted send also requires a non-interrupted assistant message after offer creation and before the user's response. The model interprets what those messages mean; these structural checks do not independently prove semantic agreement or that audio reached the listener.

Missing transcript evidence, interrupted responses and changes during a pending action fail closed. Stop and hangup remain available even when transcript persistence fails. Transcript ingestion itself does not classify intent. Unknown provider outcomes remain durable and are not retried. Only delivery receipts establish SMS delivery, and only verified Shopify orders establish purchases.

Product answers use freshly loaded Shopify cart/product data. Empty descriptions and unavailable shipping/return facts remain unknown; the agent may refer to the verified shop support address. There is no invented FAQ or claim of historical objection-handling success. Shopify legal-policy ingestion would require the additional `read_legal_policies` permission and is not enabled by this change.

## Alternative products and prepared checkout links

`search_products` reads a bounded selection from the connected shop, optionally below a maximum unit price. Results contain real descriptions, variants and current prices, not inferred quality or appearance claims. Publication is verified using Shopify's `publishedAt`; `onlineStoreUrl` was null even for published test-shop products. Unpublished, sold-out, subscription-only, gift-card and component-dependent variants are excluded. Search examines up to eight products and ten variants each, returns at most six choices, and explicitly reports that it is not exhaustive. More specific search terms can refine results.

`prepare_checkout` previews the complete requested selection (up to ten distinct variants, quantity 1–10 each). It never edits the abandoned checkout, purchases anything or sends a message. It revalidates variants in this shop, calculates the subtotal, and optionally verifies a merchant-approved discount against the new subtotal. Proposals belong to the current call, expire for sending after 30 minutes, and corrections supersede older proposals. Repeating an unchanged proposal preserves its review.

After presenting the proposal and receiving natural agreement plus an SMS request, the agent uses `send_checkout_link(prepared_checkout_id=..., use_discount=...)`. The existing worker-bound conversation evidence and backend contact controls apply. An uninterrupted assistant message must follow preparation and precede the current user response. The model interprets agreement; no phrase matcher was added. Selected prices and availability are checked again before reserving and dispatching the SMS. Changes require a new preview. A discount is only included with `use_discount=true`, and must be verified for this specific proposal. A previously applied discount in the abandoned cart is not copied.

The link uses Shopify's supported `/cart/variant:quantity` permalink with a random proposal identifier in cart attributes. Legacy cart permalinks are used because the newer `/buy` format does not yet support cart attributes needed for attribution. Customer email/address/phone are not embedded in the URL. Shopify collects or autofills buyer details, validates final pricing, shipping and taxes, and creates the actual checkout when the link opens. No order or payment is created by preparing the link. Store password protection still applies.

SMS deduplication is per verified target, with a maximum of three distinct messages per abandoned checkout. This permits an explicitly requested alternative after an original link was already sent, while identical targets and unknown dispatch outcomes are never resent automatically. Signed Shopify order webhooks associate a new checkout back to recovery only when the random proposal, same shop, recipient phone, selected variants, sent SMS and seven-day call window match. Existing paid/test/refund exclusions still govern reported revenue. This is linked-sale attribution, not a claim of incremental revenue.

Verification: local PostgreSQL tests cover preview → review → SMS → paid-order attribution, concurrent duplicate requests, changed selections/prices/inventory, contact stops, cross-tenant IDs, correction during dispatch, and ambiguous provider outcomes. Nineteen offline SDK checks and 24 real reasoning-model cases passed (`evaluate_recovery.py` plus `evaluate_catalog.py`); the latter use synthetic tools with no business effects. A real browser opened the alternative Hydrogen snowboard checkout at USD 600 with neither the Liquid item nor the old discount; no payment was submitted. The subsequent owner telephone test completed: the agent found the Hydrogen snowboard, prepared it at USD 600, obtained agreement to the presented selection and sent one SMS with a signed delivered receipt. Opening that exact SMS link after the visitor password showed Hydrogen, no Liquid item and no discount. No payment was submitted. The call ended and temporary test services/settings were cleaned up.

The prompt now treats a broad quality concern as a conversational sales objection: respond positively and directly, use a relevant verified benefit when available, and clarify the specific concern only when unclear. ## Permanent contact blocklist

The existing `recovery_contacts` row is the durable suppression record; no duplicate blocklist table or temporary expiry is introduced. Its key is derived from the shop ID and normalized phone number. `stop_contact`, refusal/wrong-person outcomes and the merchant stop action persist `stopped_at`, revoke contact permission, cancel pending actions and request termination of active calls for that number. A new cart, different email, import, OAuth reconnect or renewed consent does not clear the stop. There is no automatic unblock or merchant consent override. Stops are per shop, not shared between unrelated merchants; a different phone number is a different contact.

Newly imported checkouts for stopped contacts now also display `stopped`, while completed purchases retain `converted`. The backend independently rejects subsequent calls and SMS even if a checkout status is stale. Retention removes phone/evidence/history but preserves the suppression key and original stop reason so a returning number still matches. Already dispatched SMS cannot be recalled; pending messages are canceled and authorization is rechecked before sending.

Agent-initiated stops and refusal outcomes now resolve the contact from the persisted call ID, scoped to its shop and checkout, rather than trusting the checkout's current phone. This prevents a phone edit during a conversation from blocking the replacement recipient while leaving the actual caller unblocked. Merchant stops continue to target the selected checkout's current contact. The internal route uses the token-bound call ID, never a caller-selected phone or request-body contact ID. Repeated stops preserve the original timestamp/reason.

Regression coverage includes new carts after retention, normalized phone formatting, changed email, repeated imports, consent renewal rejection, blocked calls/SMS, pending action cancellation, shop isolation, foreign-call rejection and both stop paths after an in-call phone change. Semantic cases cover future-contact refusals in DE/EN while preserving the distinction between an opt-out, thinking time and declining a discount. This is backend suppression, without a separate blocklist management screen or unblock endpoint.

Verification: all local recovery database/unit suites, 19 offline SDK checks and 38 real-model scenarios passed. An ambiguous existing “Verarbeitung” question triggered an order-status lookup in one run; the product-quality fixture now explicitly says “Verarbeitung des Shirts” and passes. Review/security checked scoped call lookup, parameterized writes, preserved suppression records and no public unblock or arbitrary-recipient control. No real customer or owner opt-out was performed for this change.

## Compact voice prompt, 2026-11-03

Following the [official GPT-Live prompting guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/live-prompting), the voice instructions now focus on conversational style, product grounding, the sales objective and handoff conditions. Repeated examples and detailed procedures were removed from the spoken layer; the resolved backend instructions, tools, model, voices and side-effect checks are unchanged. The core voice prompt is roughly 55% shorter (previously 1,138 whitespace-separated words).

The voice layer retains the required order for price objections (approved discount, customer response, then alternatives), verified product identity, natural acceptance before a discounted link, no unsolicited messages, respect for thinking time and durable contact stops. Separate backchannel, interruption and delegation sections distinguish brief listening cues from repetitive acknowledgements and work announcements. They permit conversational variation without a mandatory question or fixed answer length.

Verification: 21 offline SDK checks passed, and a direct comparison confirmed the resolved backend instructions are identical. Review covered the assembled demo/phone identity and language prompts, all retained conversational requirements and the existing tool boundaries. The existing reasoning-model evaluations would not test the changed voice layer, so they were not rerun as evidence of spoken naturalness. The owner will perform the listening test after deployment.
